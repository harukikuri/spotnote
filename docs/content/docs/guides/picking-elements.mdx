## Selection mode

In **selection mode**, an outline follows your cursor and a label shows the element under it: tag, first class, and `file:line` with a framework logo (React, Solid, Vue, Svelte, or a generic `</>` when Spotnote can't tell which JSX framework compiles the file). Clicking an element opens the note panel instead of reaching your app.

There are two ways in:

- **Open the launcher** (the round button in a corner). Selection mode stays on until you pause it or close the launcher.
- **Hold `Alt`** (`⌥ Option` on a Mac). Selection mode lasts while the key is down. Use it for a quick pick while you're using your app normally.

While the note panel is open, clicks on the page still don't reach your app. Clicking another element moves the panel to it; clicking empty space closes the panel. See [Notes](/guides/notes) for what happens to text you haven't sent.

## The launcher

The launcher is a round button in a corner of the page. It always starts closed when the page loads.

- **Open** (`+`): selection mode starts, the button turns yellow, and your pins appear.
- **Close** (`×`): selection mode ends and the pins hide.

Opening it fans three buttons out along an arc:

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Pause | Turns selection mode off (and back on) without closing the launcher, so you can use your app with your pins still showing. The launcher turns grey while paused. |
| Notes | Opens the list of your notes, numbered like their pins. See [Notes](/guides/notes#the-notes-list). |
| Settings | Opens [Settings](/guides/settings): the accent color and a light or dark theme. |

**Move it** by dragging: it snaps to the nearest corner, the arc turns to face into the page, and the corner is remembered.

## Backing out with `Esc`

`Esc` closes one thing at a time, in this order:

1. the notes list,
2. the note panel,
3. selection mode (the launcher stays open, paused).

## What a click selects

Spotnote picks the element that carries a **stamp**: the `data-spotnote` attribute the plugin adds to every element written in your source. If you click something without one (for example, markup rendered inside a dependency), the selection moves up to the nearest ancestor that has one, and the prompt says so.

See [Lists & components](/guides/lists-and-components) for repeated elements and component boundaries.
